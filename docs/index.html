Why I made it

I am an art director. Almost every week I send Figma designs to animators. And every time it was the same story.

Export a PDF. Open it in Illustrator. Delete the clipping masks nobody asked for, the black masks, the frame clips. Rename every layer, because they are all called "Group". Then export a separate SVG, because the text came out outlined.

It took a lot of time. With complex or raster graphics it often went wrong. And with ten frames it was a nightmare. So I built this, for me and for my animators.

What you get

01

One layer per element

Every element of the frame is its own top-level layer, with its Figma name. After Effects sees them as separate layers.

02

Clean masks

The useless clipping masks go away. Empty groups too. Real masks stay, and they get a clear name.

03

Text your way

Editable, outlined, or both. With both, the outlined one is visible and the editable one waits hidden under it.

04

Many frames at once

Select ten frames, get ten .ai files. Into your default folder, or you pick the folder at the end.

05

Safe names

Layer names without accents and odd characters, so expressions and scripts in After Effects don't break.

06

Stays on your computer

Nothing goes to the internet. The plugin talks only to Dunk, on your own machine.

One rule for layers: the direct children of the frame become the layers. If you want a group split into its children, start its name with +, for example +Icons.

Download Dunk

Dunk is the small helper app. It sits in your menu bar and does the Illustrator part. You need it, because a Figma plugin can't write .ai files by itself.

Install, in 4 steps

  1. Open the .dmg and drag Dunk to Applications.
  2. Dunk is not signed yet, so the first time macOS stops it. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. You do this only once.
  3. Start Dunk. A small icon appears in the menu bar.
  4. On the first job macOS asks if Dunk can control Adobe Illustrator. Click Allow.

Why not signed? Signing costs a yearly fee, and this is a free side project. The code is open on GitHub, so you can check what it does.

Use it in Figma

  1. Install Alleyoop from the Figma Community and open it.
  2. Select one or more frames.
  3. Choose the text mode and where to save.
  4. Click Send to Illustrator. The files land in Desktop/Alleyoop.

In After Effects, import the .ai as Composition – Retain Layer Sizes. Every layer comes in as its own layer.

Questions

The plugin says "Dunk is not running"

Start Dunk from Applications. If it still doesn't connect, open the Dunk menu and click Diagnostics. It writes a short report.

What doesn't work perfectly?

Background blur and some effects are exported as images. A Figma mask on frame level can't always stay a mask, the plugin warns you before. Multi-line text is one text frame per line.

Does it work in the browser version of Figma?

The desktop app is recommended. Some browsers block a website from talking to an app on your computer.

Which Illustrator?

Illustrator 2024, 2025 or 2026. If you have more versions, you can pick one in the Dunk menu.

Something broke. What now?

Please open an issue on GitHub. Every error message ends with the path of the log file, attach it. I read every one.
